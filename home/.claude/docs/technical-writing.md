# Technical Writing Principles

Core writing principles for all technical documents. Load this file when producing design docs, one-pagers, or any technical communication.

---

## FOCUS Framework [CRITICAL]

All communication must follow:

- **Focused**: Address the issue, the whole issue, and nothing but the issue.
- **Organized**: Systematically present information and ideas.
- **Clear**: Make each word count.
- **Understanding**: Know your audience and their expectations.
- **Supported**: Use logic and evidence to make your points.

---

## Plain Language [CRITICAL]

### Be Clear
- Avoid jargon and overusing acronyms.
- Use second person ("you") rather than "we."

### Be Concise
- Remove unnecessary words.
- Sentences: 20 words or fewer.
- Paragraphs: 7 sentences or fewer.

### Be Specific
- Only include information the reader must know.
- Use words with precise meaning.

---

## Active Voice [CRITICAL]

Prefer active voice. Use passive only when the subject is unknown or you intend to emphasize the object.

| Passive | Active |
|---------|--------|
| The mat was sat on by the cat. | The cat sat on the mat. |
| The flags weren't parsed by the Mungifier. | The Mungifier didn't parse the flags. |
| A wrapper is generated by the Op registration process. | The Op registration process generates a wrapper. |

---

## Sentence Construction [IMPORTANT]

- State your purpose early (bottom line up front).
- Avoid smothered verbs — use specific verbs instead of general verb + extra words.
- Vary sentence length for rhythm.
- Use parallel construction in lists and series.
- Avoid redundancy and word doublings.
- Keep related words together; place modifiers next to what they modify.
- Stick to one tense throughout.
- Place important words at the end of sentences.
- Opening phrases must refer to the grammatical subject — avoid dangling modifiers.

---

## Grammar Essentials [IMPORTANT]

- Add "'s" for singular possessives, even after "s" (Charles's, Burns's).
- Use serial comma (red, white, and blue).
- Place commas before conjunctions joining independent clauses.
- Place modifiers next to what they modify.
- Match subject-verb and pronoun-antecedent number agreement.
- Enclose non-restrictive (removable) expressions with commas.
- Don't fragment sentences — don't use a period where a comma belongs.

---

## Paragraph Construction [CRITICAL]

One paragraph = one topic = one unit of focus.

- Begin with the topic sentence.
- Supporting sentences expand, clarify, illustrate, or explain.
- 3–7 sentences per paragraph.
- End in conformity — the final sentence reinforces the main point. Do NOT wander off to a digression.

---

## Concise Writing [IMPORTANT]

### Principles
1. Omit needless words.
2. Prefer simple words.
3. Use simple subjects.
4. Use adjectives/adverbs frugally.

### Phrases to Eliminate
- "Note that", "It should be noted that", "It is important to realize", "So-called"

### Common Substitutions

| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| due to the fact that | because |
| a large number of | many |
| at this point in time | now |
| in the event that | if |
| prior to | before |
| subsequent to | after |
| methodology | method |
| utilize | use |
| in order to | to |
| in accordance with | per |
| with regard to | about |
| at the present time | now |
| for the purpose of | for, to |

For a comprehensive list, consult the wordy words reference files:
- `wordy-words-a-f.md`
- `wordy-words-g-p.md`
- `wordy-words-q-z.md`

### Words to Avoid

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|-------|-------------|
| "the fact that" | because, since, although |
| "very" | use a stronger word |
| "kind of", "sort of" | rather, somewhat |
| "interesting", "interestingly" | make the content speak for itself |
| "less" (for countables) | fewer |
| "like" (before clauses) | as |
| "different than" | different from |
| "He is a man who" | he |
| "along these lines" | be specific |

## Tone [IMPORTANT]

- Be polite; avoid anger, sarcasm, excessive criticism.
- Be positive; say what "is," not what "isn't."
- Use pronouns (you, we, us) to create rapport — use "I" sparingly.
- Be specific and concrete.

---

## Credibility [IMPORTANT]

- Research thoroughly; get facts right.
- Don't bluff — say "I don't know" when you don't.
- Get easy things right (spelling, dates, grammar).
- Demonstrate goodwill in your tone.
- Acknowledge mistakes and move on.

---

## Audience Analysis [CRITICAL]

- Know your primary receiver, secondary receiver, key decision makers, and gatekeepers.
- Tailor jargon to your audience.
- Be inclusive and avoid stereotypes.

---

## Evidence Quality [IMPORTANT]

When supporting claims, verify:
- **(Trust)** Is the source authoritative?
- **(Accuracy)** Is the information free from error?
- **(Precision)** Is the information appropriately specific?
- **(Relevance)** Does the evidence support your claim?
- **(Sufficiency)** Is there enough evidence?

---

## Logical Arguments [IMPORTANT]

### Structure
- **Claim**: Your position or recommendation.
- **Evidence**: Data/facts supporting the claim.
- **Warrants**: Assumptions linking evidence to claim.
- **Qualifications**: Conditions that limit the claim.

### Fallacies to Avoid
Ad Hominem, Straw Man, Red Herring, False Cause, Hasty Generalization, Circular Reasoning, False Dilemma, Stacked Evidence, Slippery Slope, Appeal to Emotion, Faulty Analogy, Non Sequitur.

---

## Colons, Semicolons, and Dashes [IMPORTANT]

| Situation | Use | Why |
|-----------|-----|-----|
| Joining equal clauses | Semicolon | Partners; interdependent ideas |
| First clause sets up second | Colon | Arrow pointing forward |
| Dramatic reveal or emphasis | Em dash | Spotlight; informal energy |
| Aside you want to highlight | Em dashes (pair) | Draws attention |
| Aside you want to downplay | Parentheses | Whispered; skippable |
| Ranges or scores | En dash | Replaces "to" |

Limit to 2 em dashes per sentence.

### Emphasis Continuum for Asides

From least to most emphatic:
1. **Parentheses** — whispered; de-emphasized; skippable
2. **Commas** — neutral; natural flow; least intrusive
3. **Em dashes** — spotlight; dramatic; draws attention

---

## Three-Pass Editing [RECOMMENDED]

1. **First pass**: Big picture — purpose, introduction, conclusion, overall flow.
2. **Second pass**: Paragraphs — unity, topic sentences, supporting ideas, transitions.
3. **Third pass**: Sentences and words — active voice, clarity, grammar, spelling.

---

## E-mails [IMPORTANT]

- Lead with the purpose or request.
- One subject per e-mail.
- Short paragraphs, especially the first.
- End with a clear call to action or next steps.
- For replies: address all points raised, in order.

---

## Code Comments [IMPORTANT]

- Explain *why*, not just *what*.
- Document edge cases and known limitations.
- Keep comments current with code changes.
- Write for the maintainer who comes after you.

---

## API and Package Documentation [IMPORTANT]

- Start with a clear overview of what the package does.
- Provide working code examples (not theoretical snippets).
- Document all public interfaces completely.
- Include error handling and edge cases.
- Organize around user tasks, not internal structure.

---

## Quality Checklist [CRITICAL]

Before finalizing any technical communication:

- [ ] Purpose stated early and clearly
- [ ] Audience needs addressed
- [ ] Active voice throughout
- [ ] Sentences average 20 words or fewer
- [ ] Paragraphs focus on single topics
- [ ] Technical terms defined or linked
- [ ] Examples are complete and tested
- [ ] Grammar and spelling correct
- [ ] Formatting consistent
- [ ] All claims supported with evidence
